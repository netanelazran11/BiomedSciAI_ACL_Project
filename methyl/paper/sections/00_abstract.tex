DNA methylation is a stable, information-rich epigenetic signal that reflects tissue identity
and serves as a powerful biomarker for disease diagnosis and biological age assessment.
However, established predictors are trained one endpoint at a time, so shared structure is
relearned for each task while much of the unlabelled corpus remains unused. Here we introduce
\method{}, a Transformer-based foundation model pretrained without
phenotype supervision on 169{,}120 profiles from diverse tissues and studies, represented at
49{,}156 CpGs. We adapt Whole-Cell Expression Decoding to methylation as the Whole-CpG
Encoder--Decoder (WCED). WCED encodes
two independently sampled partial views of each profile and requires their CLS representations
to reconstruct CpGs absent from the corresponding inputs, while symmetric InfoNCE aligns views
of the same sample and separates different samples. On held-out profiles, \method{} retained
sample identity across partial measurements, reconstructed unseen CpGs substantially better than
population and representation-disruption controls, and captured age- and tissue-associated
variation without labels. Fine-tuning reduced median absolute age error by 0.59 years (95\% CI,
0.34--0.82) relative to a sample- and split-matched MethylGPT pipeline and achieved better
point estimates than ElasticNetCV. These findings establish \method{} as a reusable DNA methylation
foundation model while revealing study-specific structure as a central challenge for cross-cohort
generalization.
